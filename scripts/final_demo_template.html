A Multi-Agent LLM Framework for Explainable Algorithmic Trading on NSE-Listed Equities

How a decision is made

Four agents each give a stance from −1 (bearish) to +1 (bullish) with a confidence, or abstain when they have no evidence. A trader combines them, and a rule-based risk manager has the final say on position size.

1 · DataAdjusted prices, news headlines and annual reports, each visible only from the date it was public.
2 · Four agentsTechnical (LSTM with attention), sentiment (LLM on headlines), regime (trend and volatility), fundamentals.
3 · DebateOn contested days an LLM scores a bull case against a bear case.
4 · TraderConfidence-weighted average of the stances gives one score; positive means buy.
5 · Risk managerDeterministic rules: volatility targeting, 20% cap per stock, 100% cap on the book.
Technical agent

Peephole LSTM cells and a temporal-attention layer, written from scratch in PyTorch, trained on 30-day sequences of price, volume and indicator features to predict the probability that the stock rises from the next open to the following open. A 2×2 ablation (peephole on/off × attention on/off) with three seeds each isolates the architecture's contribution. Predictions are averaged over seeds; picking the best seed would be selecting on the test set. Every trading configuration uses the LSTM-TAL variant (attention without peepholes); no variant differs from chance, so the choice does not change any conclusion.

Sentiment agent

An instruction-tuned LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B locally; Qwen2.5-3B for the final comparison) labels each headline Good, Bad or Unknown for the stock. The backend reads the three labels' first-token logits in one forward pass instead of generating text, so the output is deterministic and never unparseable. Daily views average the headline scores; the agent abstains on days without news.

Regime agent

Four causal, equally weighted votes: price against its 200-day average, 60-day relative strength against the Nifty, position in the 52-week range, and (optionally) Nifty momentum. Confidence is cut in high-volatility states.

Fundamentals agent (new for the final evaluation)

Uses only annual statements, each treated as unknown until 61 days after the fiscal year end, one day past the SEBI LODR Regulation 33 deadline for audited results, so the agent can be late but never early. Net-profit growth, revenue growth, return on equity and change in ROE are z-scored across the universe on each decision date using only reports public on that date. Limitations: the source shows restated figures, and no valuation ratio is used.

Debate, trader and risk manager

On contested days a bull and a bear researcher argue from the agents' findings; the LLM scores the strength of each case and the residual moves the score by a fixed 35% weight. The trader takes a confidence-weighted mean of stances with no learned stacker. The deterministic risk manager applies a confidence floor, 20% volatility targeting, a drawdown brake, a 20% per-name cap and a 100% gross cap; idle cash earns a 6% risk-free rate. Two opt-in cost-aware components were added for the final evaluation: an edge-against-cost gate and a 5% no-trade band (entries and exits are never banded).

Evaluation protocol and cost model

Purged, embargoed walk-forward: 3-year training windows, 6-month test windows, a 10-day embargo, 16 folds. Positions are decided at the close of day t and executed at the open of t+1. Costs follow the 2026 delivery schedule: 0.1% STT each side, 0.015% stamp duty on buys, NSE and SEBI charges with GST, and 5 bps slippage, about 32 bps round trip. Sharpe ratios are in excess of 6%, with stationary block-bootstrap 95% intervals; differences against Buy&Hold are Holm-corrected across all configurations, and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio accounts for the number of strategies tried.

Run it yourself

The trader, the risk manager, the cost model and the backtest are re-executed in your browser on the 19,110 recorded out-of-sample agent opinions and the real next-day returns. The LSTM and the LLM cannot run in a browser, so their recorded outputs are the inputs; the debate step is not available here.

Limitations

Statistical power

The bootstrap test reliably detects a Sharpe difference of about 1.0 over this window, not smaller. “No significant difference” is not “proven identical”.

Model scale

No hosted frontier model was available; the comparison is 1.5B against 3B open models. A frontier model could behave differently.

Survivorship

Both universes are today's large caps, which flatters Buy&Hold more than the agents.

Fundamentals and news

Disclosure-lagged but restated annual figures, with no valuation ratio. Headlines cover ten names; the extended universe has no sentiment agent.
